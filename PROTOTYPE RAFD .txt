1. Project Overview

Project Background and Challenge

Farmers around the world face increasing agricultural pressures caused by shifting temperatures, changing rainfall patterns, water shortages, extreme weather, and declining soil health.

These challenges make it difficult for farmers to choose suitable crop rotation strategies that protect soil health, conserve water, and strengthen the long-term resilience of their farms.

RAFD addresses this challenge by developing an intelligent agricultural decision-support platform that helps farmers understand their local agricultural conditions and explore suitable crop rotation strategies using reliable environmental data and farm-specific information.

Project Idea

RAFD is an intelligent agricultural decision-support platform designed for Saudi Arabia.

It combines NASA Earth observations, local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer-provided information and priorities to help farmers:

	•	Understand environmental and agricultural conditions affecting their farms.
	•	Identify potential challenges related to drought, humidity, soil, climate, and water.
	•	Evaluate crop suitability under changing environmental conditions.
	•	Explore and compare crop rotation strategies.
	•	Identify strategies that support soil health, improve water-use efficiency, and strengthen agricultural resilience.

Main Problem

Farmers face changing environmental conditions and agricultural pressures that make it difficult to decide which crops to grow and how to organize crop rotations while protecting soil health and managing limited water resources.

RAFD Solution

RAFD transforms NASA Earth observation data and local agricultural information into practical decision support.

The platform analyzes available environmental conditions, soil information, crop requirements, and farmer priorities to help users explore and compare crop rotation strategies that may support:

	•	Better soil health management.
	•	More efficient water use.
	•	Improved crop suitability.
	•	Adaptation to changing climate conditions.
	•	Long-term agricultural resilience.

The platform does not automatically guarantee improved soil health or crop productivity. It provides data-supported insights and strategies that farmers can evaluate before making agricultural decisions.

Project Data Inputs

The system combines:

	•	Available NASA Earth observation and climate data.
	•	Official Saudi agricultural datasets.
	•	Local soil information, when available.
	•	Crop characteristics and agricultural requirements.
	•	User-provided farm information and priorities.

Project Tagline

Smart Agriculture. Smarter Decisions.

Core Message

RAFD does not simply show farmers agricultural problems. It helps them understand the conditions affecting their farms and explore practical crop and crop rotation strategies based on available data.

Main Project Objective

Develop a feasible decision-support tool that uses NASA Earth observations, local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to help farmers explore crop rotation strategies that could support soil health, conserve water, and strengthen agricultural resilience under changing environmental conditions.

2. Visual Identity and Design System

Approved Color Palette

Use the following colors as the primary visual identity:

	•	Forest Green: Main headings, navigation bar, primary buttons, and brand elements.
	•	Leaf Green: Secondary buttons, positive indicators, and interactive elements.
	•	Earth Brown: Soil-related elements, agricultural sections, and environmental indicators.
	•	Sand Beige: Cards, secondary sections, and warm backgrounds.
	•	Warm Cream: Main website background.
	•	Soft Sage Green: Form backgrounds, secondary cards, and information panels.
	•	White: Cards, clean spaces, and content areas.
	•	Dark Gray: Main text and supporting information.

Use orange, red, and yellow only for warning and risk indicators.

Design Direction

	•	Modern and professional interface.
	•	Clean and organized layout.
	•	Scientific and technology-driven agricultural identity.
	•	Soft rounded cards.
	•	Subtle shadows.
	•	Comfortable spacing.
	•	Clear data visualizations.
	•	Consistent icons.
	•	Fully responsive design for desktop and mobile.
	•	Full Arabic right-to-left support.
	•	Language switcher for Arabic and English.

Design Restrictions

	•	Do not use photographs or images in the prototype.
	•	Avoid crowded backgrounds.
	•	Avoid excessive bright colors.
	•	Do not make the interface look like an online store.
	•	Do not place all information on a single page.
	•	Never present fictional values as real scientific data.

Use icons, maps, charts, and visual UI elements without photographic images.

7. Data Sources

Use only the following sources in the core prototype.

NASA Data

NASA POWER:

	•	Air temperature.
	•	Precipitation.
	•	Solar radiation.
	•	Available climate variables.
	•	Climate comparisons between regions.

NASA GPM:

	•	Precipitation observations when needed.
	•	Rainfall comparisons over time.
	•	Support for rainfall-deficit analysis.

NASA SMAP:

	•	Soil moisture information at the spatial scale supported by the product.
	•	Never present it as a direct measurement of a small farm field.

NASA Earthdata:

	•	NASA data access portal.
	•	Not an independent sensor or measurement product.

Official Saudi Data Sources

	•	Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture: Agricultural statistics, crop information, and water-related information.
	•	General Authority for Statistics: Relevant agricultural and geographic statistics.
	•	National Center for Meteorology: Climate information and meteorological observations.
	•	National Center for Vegetation Cover Development and Combating Desertification: Vegetation, desertification, and afforestation information.

Data Display Requirements

Every displayed data item must include:

	•	Variable name.
	•	Value.
	•	Unit.
	•	Geographic location.
	•	Time period.
	•	Data source.
	•	Latest available update date.

Never present fictional data as real data.

If data cannot be retrieved, clearly display a data-unavailable message instead of inventing a value.

11. Agricultural Problem Analysis

The platform must analyze five main agricultural challenges:

	•	Drought.
	•	Humidity and Soil Moisture.
	•	Soil.
	•	Climate.
	•	Water.

For every problem, use the following structure:

	•	Problem description.
	•	Related indicators.
	•	Data used.
	•	Possible cause.
	•	Potential agricultural impact.
	•	Risk level.
	•	Suggested solutions.
	•	Recommended next action.

Drought

Indicators:

	•	Rainfall below a selected reference period.
	•	High temperatures.
	•	Persistent dry periods.

Solutions:

	•	Review the irrigation schedule.
	•	Consider lower-water-demand crops where suitable.
	•	Improve irrigation efficiency.
	•	Use suitable soil mulching practices.
	•	Review planting timing.

Humidity and Soil Moisture

Clearly distinguish between atmospheric humidity and soil moisture.

Possible conditions:

	•	High atmospheric humidity.
	•	Low soil moisture.
	•	Excessive soil moisture.
	•	Rapid moisture fluctuations.

Solutions:

	•	Adjust irrigation timing based on available data.
	•	Improve drainage when waterlogging is confirmed.
	•	Monitor ventilation and plant density.
	•	Avoid excessive irrigation.

Never infer soil moisture directly from atmospheric humidity.

Soil

Information:

	•	User-provided soil type.
	•	Laboratory soil results, if available.
	•	Available official soil information.

Solutions:

	•	Conduct soil testing when information is missing.
	•	Add organic matter based on test results.
	•	Improve drainage when needed.
	•	Apply suitable crop rotation.
	•	Reduce soil erosion.

Do not claim to know soil salinity or nutrient levels without supporting data.

Climate

Indicators:

	•	Temperature.
	•	Precipitation.
	•	Solar radiation.
	•	Seasonal changes.

Solutions:

	•	Adjust planting timing.
	•	Select more suitable crops.
	•	Use crop protection methods when necessary.
	•	Review irrigation schedules.

Water

Information:

	•	Water source.
	•	Available water quantity.
	•	Estimated crop water requirements.
	•	Irrigation method.

Solutions:

	•	Improve irrigation efficiency.
	•	Schedule irrigation.
	•	Compare crops by water requirements.
	•	Reduce water losses.
	•	Consider alternative crops when water is limited.

12. Farm Action Center

This is one of the platform’s most important features.

The system must not simply send an alert about high temperatures or low rainfall.

Every alert must provide a practical solution.

Alert Card Structure

Each alert must include:

	•	Alert title.
	•	Risk level.
	•	Reason for the alert.
	•	Supporting data.
	•	Potential impact.
	•	Suggested solution.
	•	Practical implementation steps.
	•	What the farmer should monitor next.

Example: High Temperature Alert

Alert:

High temperatures.

Reason:

Observed temperatures are above the selected crop reference threshold for the current growth stage.

Potential impact:

Increased heat stress and potentially higher water demand.

Suggested actions:

	•	Review the irrigation schedule.
	•	Consider irrigation during cooler periods when suitable.
	•	Monitor soil moisture using field measurements.
	•	Consider appropriate soil mulching.
	•	Evaluate crop suitability under current climate conditions.

Next action:

Open irrigation settings and enter available water quantity.

Alerts must be based on actual data or clearly documented rules. Display confidence level and missing information.

Strategy 2:

A plan focused on water-use efficiency.

Strategy 3:

A plan focused on crop diversity and soil management.

Each Strategy Must Display

	•	Crop sequence.
	•	Crop duration.
	•	Reason for the sequence.
	•	Relative water requirements.
	•	Expected soil-management benefits.
	•	Advantages.
	•	Limitations.
	•	Additional data required before adoption.

Add a comparison table to help users evaluate the three plans.

Use available farm information, environmental conditions, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities where possible.

Do not claim that a rotation plan automatically improves soil. Recommendations must be linked to crop characteristics and available information.

15. Solar Aqua Simulator

Objective

Introduce an innovative concept connecting solar energy with water solutions for dry agricultural environments.

Concept

A conceptual simulation of a system using:

	•	Solar energy.
	•	Atmospheric water harvesting.
	•	Condensation.
	•	Water collection.
	•	Water storage.
	•	Agricultural irrigation.

Simulation Interface

Display the system as a visual sequence:

	•	Capture solar energy.
	•	Operate the atmospheric water harvesting system.
	•	Condense water vapor.
	•	Collect water.
	•	Store water.
	•	Use water for irrigation.

Allow users to adjust:

	•	Solar radiation level.
	•	Temperature.
	•	Relative humidity.
	•	Operating duration.

Display estimated or illustrative output only if a documented engineering equation and suitable inputs are available.

If reliable performance equations or data are unavailable, keep the simulation conceptual and do not generate quantitative output.

Display a clear note:

This is a conceptual demonstration, not a deployed device or a field-validated system.
